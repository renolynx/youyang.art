Inheriting a Mountain

Minnesota is a place with no mountains.

In the 1890s a jade mountain carved for the Qianlong emperor was looted from the Old Summer Palace in Beijing. It passed through the hands of an American legation secretary, crossed the ocean, and was bought at auction by T. B. Walker — the lumber baron whose private collection became this state's first art museum. The jade mountain is still here, in the permanent collection of the Minneapolis Institute of Art, a few miles from where I have been living.

I made my own mountain out of clay, carried it onto the frozen lake the settlers renamed Lake Harriet, and sat down with it. The Dakota called this water Bdé Umáŋ. The piece was shown at the Minneapolis College of Art and Design as part of a Jerome Fellowship.
